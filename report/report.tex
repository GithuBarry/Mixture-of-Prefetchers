\title{\textbf{Two-Expert Epoch Coordination for L2 Prefetching on Athena}\\
\large A Scope-Locked Stage 1 Baseline and Stage 2 Policy Optimization}

\author{15-740 Computer Architecture\\
Carnegie Mellon University}

\date{April 28, 2026}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
This report delivers a complete Stage 1 baseline and Stage 2 policy optimization
for epoch-level coordination of two L2 prefetcher experts (Pythia and SPP+PPF) in
the Athena simulator. Stage 1 establishes a reproducible negative result: the
\textsc{MoPLite} coordinator does not beat the pair-best single expert in geomean IPC
across 24 benchmark traces (0.9188$\times$ held-out vs.\ pair-best single).
Epoch-trace diagnostics identify two structural failure modes: \textit{both-off overuse}
(90--97\% of epochs on hard traces) and \textit{insufficient winner isolation}.
Stage 2 introduces \textsc{OpenEvolve} (router type 5), which applies three targeted
fixes—an anti-off gate (E1), winner isolation (E2), and a squared-score budget split
(E3). On the 10-trace search subset, \textsc{OpenEvolve} improves geomean from 0.968$\times$
to \textbf{0.978$\times$} (+0.98 pp), matching \textsc{AthenaMAB} without online learning.
On the 7-trace held-out split at final-mode windows (20M/50M instructions),
\textsc{OpenEvolve} reaches \textbf{0.924$\times$} (+0.46 pp over Stage 1 \textsc{MoPLite}),
crossing 1.0$\times$ on 3 of 7 traces. A 40-iteration automated OpenEvolve search
(claude-haiku-4-5, CMU AI Gateway) is also reported; the best evolved candidate
generalizes poorly due to scout-set overfitting, leaving the manual seed as the
strongest fixed-rule result.
\end{abstract}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

The central question is deliberately narrow: can a small epoch-based controller
coordinate exactly two L2 prefetcher experts under explicit traffic and usefulness
constraints, and beat strong baselines without sacrificing scientific clarity?

The scope is fixed throughout: single-core Athena, L2C-only coordination,
off-chip prediction (OCP) disabled, instruction-counted epochs, and the committed
expert pair \textbf{Pythia + SPP+PPF}. This scope frames the contribution as a
baseline study of \emph{coordination itself}, not a broad architecture search.

The primary performance comparator is \textbf{pair-best single expert}:
$\max(\text{IPC}_{\text{Pythia}},\, \text{IPC}_{\text{SPP+PPF}})$ per trace per run
group. Coordination is only meaningful if it can beat the stronger constituent.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Action Space and Score Function}

Each coordinated run changes only the epoch-level decision rule. The action space
is identical across all routers: \{both off, expert 0 only, expert 1 only, both on
with shared budget\}. The \textsc{MoPLite} score for expert $i$ is:

\[
  s_i = w_a \cdot \frac{\text{acc}_i}{100}
       + w_c \cdot \text{cov}_i
       - w_t \cdot \text{traf}_i
\]

where $\text{acc}_i$ is prefetch accuracy (\%), $\text{cov}_i = \text{useful}_i /
\text{retired\_insts}$, and $\text{traf}_i = \text{issued}_i / \text{total\_issued}$.
A hard floor zeroes the score when $\text{acc}_i < 30\%$.
Default weights: $w_a = 1.0$, $w_c = 0.25$, $w_t = 1.0$.

\subsection{Coordinator Family}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textsc{FixedSplit}] Always both on; fixed budget ratio (50/50).
  \item[\textsc{WinnerTakeAll}] Full budget to the higher-scoring expert.
  \item[\textsc{RandomRouter}] Random single-expert choice per epoch.
  \item[\textsc{OneShotFit}] Estimate winner in early epochs, then freeze.
  \item[\textsc{MoPLite}] Route by score signs: both-off / single winner / proportional split.
  \item[\textsc{OpenEvolve}] Stage 2 router with E1--E3 fixes (§\ref{sec:stage2}).
  \item[\textsc{AthenaMAB}] Upstream Athena built-in: per-arm discounted-UCB reward estimation.
\end{description}

\textsc{AthenaMAB} is the main prior-method comparator. The key distinction from
\textsc{OpenEvolve} is that \textsc{AthenaMAB} uses online reward estimation with
per-arm discounted counts; \textsc{OpenEvolve} uses a fixed, auditable rule.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Split.} The official split is frozen in
\texttt{data/splits/official\_v1.json}: 17 train traces and 7 held-out traces,
benchmark-family-aware (no family split across train and held-out).

\paragraph{Instruction windows.} Search-side: 5M warmup + 10M simulation.
Held-out (final): 20M warmup + 50M simulation.

\paragraph{Dataset.} The Stage 1 merged dataset contains 230 runs across
24 traces: 24 baselines, 96 single-expert runs, 86 router runs, 24 builtin
coordinator runs.

\paragraph{Benchmark suite.} 24 traces from SPEC CPU (10), PARSEC (5),
LIGRA graph workloads (5), and CVP-2 secret traces (4).

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Every run is identified by a \texttt{run\_group\_id},
\texttt{git\_revision}, host, seed, and full simulator flag string, stamped in an
append-only \texttt{manifest.jsonl}. Figures and tables are regenerated
deterministically from \texttt{data/processed/runs.csv}.

\section{Stage 1 Results}
\label{sec:stage1}

\subsection{Speedup vs.\ No-Prefetch}

Against a no-prefetch baseline, some coordinators deliver small gains on the
training split. \textsc{AthenaMAB} reaches 1.0095$\times$ (train) and 1.0378$\times$
(held-out); \textsc{MoPLite} reaches 0.9986$\times$ (train) and 0.9974$\times$
(held-out). Gains are small, split-dependent, and do not identify \textsc{MoPLite}
as the strongest rule.

\subsection{Speedup vs.\ Pair-Best Single Expert}

Table~\ref{tab:stage1_main} shows the decisive comparison. No coordinator reaches
1.0$\times$ geomean against the pair-best single expert on either split.

\textsc{MoPLite} wins 6 of 17 train traces and only 1 of 7 held-out traces above

\subsection{Expert Win Regions}

The single prefetchers have genuinely different win profiles, confirming coordination
is a real (non-degenerate) problem. On the 17-trace train split: MLOP wins 8 traces,
Pythia 5, SMS 2, SPP+PPF 2. On held-out: Pythia wins 3, SMS 3, MLOP 1.
The MoPLite failure is therefore not caused by indistinguishable experts—it is caused
by a weak action policy.

\subsection{Failure-Mode Diagnostics}

Epoch-trace analysis identifies two root causes:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Both-off overuse.} On \texttt{429.mcf-192B}, MoPLite spends 93.3\%
    of epochs in action 0 (both off). On \texttt{secret\_compute\_fp\_105}, both-off
    rate is 54.3\%. Traffic is suppressed even when one expert would be useful.

  \item \textbf{Weak winner isolation.} On \texttt{602.gcc\_s}, MoPLite includes the
    offline-better expert in 100\% of epochs but still loses overall—because it keeps
    both experts active (action 3) with a proportional split, diluting the winner.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Stage 2: OpenEvolve Router}
\label{sec:stage2}

\subsection{Design}

\textsc{OpenEvolve} (router type 5, \texttt{config/mop\_lite\_open\_evolve.ini})
applies three targeted fixes:

\paragraph{E1 — Anti-Off Gate.} When both scores are $\leq 0$ and the router would
otherwise go both-off, \textsc{OpenEvolve} falls back to the historically more-active
expert (higher cumulative \texttt{pref\_issued\_total}). On epoch 1 (no history),
it defaults to both-on. This eliminates cold-start suppression and chronic both-off
episodes.

\paragraph{E2 — Winner Isolation.} When one score exceeds the other by a configurable
ratio $\tau$ (\texttt{mop\_winner\_isolation\_threshold}, default 3.0), the router
routes exclusively to that expert with the full budget:
\[
  \text{action} = \begin{cases} 2 & s_0 \geq \tau \cdot s_1 \\ 1 & s_1 \geq \tau \cdot s_0 \end{cases}
\]

\paragraph{E3 — Squared-Score Budget Split.} For both-on epochs, budget is allocated
proportional to $s_i^2$ rather than $s_i$. A 2:1 score ratio yields a 4:1 budget
ratio, sharpening allocation toward the stronger expert.

because its online reward estimator handles this trace.

\subsection{Automated OpenEvolve Search}

A 40-iteration search was run using \texttt{openevolve} (v0.2.27) with
claude-haiku-4-5 as the primary LLM (CMU AI Gateway). The evaluator used a
3-trace scout set (429.mcf, 605.mcf\_s, 450.soplex) with 2M/4M windows
($\approx$2.5 min per evaluation; 40 iterations in $\approx$1.7 hours).

The best evolved candidate (\texttt{ffca3913}, iteration 24) found:
accuracy\_floor $30 \to 18$, isolation\_threshold $3.0 \to 1.8$,
coverage\_weight $0.25 \to 0.5$. It scored 1.088$\times$ on the 3-trace scout
but \textbf{0.972$\times$} on the full 10-trace evaluation—a regression of
$-$0.6 pp from the manual seed. This is a \textit{generalization failure}:
the scout set was too small and the windows too short to prevent overfitting.
The manual seed remains the strongest fixed-rule result.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Only 24 traces; domain shifts outside SPEC/PARSEC/LIGRA/CVP are unmeasured.
  \item One committed expert pair (Pythia + SPP+PPF); pair choice matters but was
    not the primary variable.
  \item \textsc{RandomRouter} run with one seed only; multi-seed averaging not done.
  \item Coordinator traffic uses a documented fallback proxy when Athena's raw
    \texttt{L2C\_prefetch\_issued} counter stays zero.
  \item Pair-best single is a postmortem oracle comparator, not a deployable policy.
  \item Automated search overfit: the 3-trace scout did not generalize to the
    10-trace evaluation; a broader scout with full-length windows is needed.
  \item The held-out success criterion ($\geq 1.0\times$ geomean) is not met.
    \texttt{secret\_compute\_fp\_105} requires online adaptation, not a fixed-rule fix.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Stage 1 establishes a reproducible two-expert L2 coordination baseline on Athena.
\textsc{MoPLite} does not beat the pair-best single expert in geomean on either
split. Epoch-trace diagnostics identify both-off overuse and weak winner isolation
as the structural root causes.

Stage 2 \textsc{OpenEvolve} (type 5) directly fixes both failure modes and improves
geomean by +0.98 pp on the search subset and +0.46 pp on the held-out split,
matching \textsc{AthenaMAB} on the search subset without online learning. The
held-out go/no-go criterion ($\geq 1.0\times$) is not met; \texttt{secret\_compute\_fp\_105}
remains the primary blocker for all fixed-rule routers.

A 40-iteration automated OpenEvolve search (claude-haiku-4-5) demonstrated that the
search infrastructure is in place but the scout set overfit. The manual seed remains
the strongest fixed-rule result. Closing the remaining gap to \textsc{AthenaMAB}
requires online adaptation or a richer score signal on strong-dominance traces.

make -C external/athena -j$(nproc)

# Stage 1: search-side + held-out

  --workers 15 --results-dir results/mop_lite_search

  --workers 15 --results-dir results/mop_lite_final

  --workers 15 --results-dir results/open_evolve_search

  openevolve/evaluator.py \
  --config openevolve/config.yaml --iterations 40

# Build dataset + figures
python3 scripts/build_dataset.py \
  --manifest results/mop_lite_search/manifest.jsonl \
  --manifest results/mop_lite_final/manifest.jsonl
python3 scripts/make_figures.py
\end{lstlisting}
